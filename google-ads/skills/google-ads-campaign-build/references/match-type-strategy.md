# Match Type Strategy

This reference defines how to approach keyword match types for new campaign builds.

## Core Philosophy: Start Tight, Expand Later

On limited budgets ($3k/month or less in competitive spaces), every click matters. Starting with exact match gives you maximum control over which searches trigger your ads. You can always loosen to phrase or broad later once you've seen what converts — but you can't get wasted spend back.

## Decision Framework

### Use Exact Match When:

The keyword IS the complete search intent. The person typing this exact phrase (or a close variant) is your ideal prospect.

**Examples:**
- "managed it services" — someone actively looking for a managed IT provider
- "cybersecurity services near me" — high commercial intent, ready to buy
- "virtual ciso services" — very specific, very qualified
- "it services for small business" — clear buyer intent

Google's exact match already handles close variants (plurals, misspellings, reordering, implied words), so you get reasonable reach without phrase match.

### Use Phrase Match When:

The core phrase is your intent anchor, but natural modifiers before/after add value without changing the intent.

**Best use cases:**

1. **Geo-modified keywords** — "it services temecula" as phrase catches "best it services temecula," "affordable it services temecula ca," "top rated it services temecula valley." The geo modifier naturally invites useful head/tail modifiers.

2. **Service category + modifier patterns** — "it service providers near me" as phrase catches "best it service providers near me," "top it service providers near me," "reliable it service providers near me."

3. **When you want to discover what modifiers people actually use** — phrase is a good "learning" match type. Run it for 2-4 weeks, mine the search terms, then promote the best-performing queries to exact match.

### Avoid Broad Match When:

- Budget is under $5k/month
- The space is competitive (high CPCs)
- You haven't built out a strong negative keyword list yet
- The user hasn't specifically asked for broad

Broad match on tight budgets in competitive spaces is how you burn through $100/day on irrelevant clicks. If the user wants broad, make sure negatives are rock-solid first.

## Match Type by Ad Group Theme (Examples)

### Local IT / "Near Me" Keywords
| Keyword | Match Type | Reasoning |
|---------|-----------|-----------|
| it services near me | Exact | Complete search intent, close variants cover the rest |
| it support near me | Exact | Same — "near me" is the full intent signal |
| it company near me | Exact | Tight control on a high-CPC term |
| it service providers near me | Phrase | Catches "best/top/reliable it service providers near me" |

### Geo-Modified Keywords
| Keyword | Match Type | Reasoning |
|---------|-----------|-----------|
| it services temecula | Phrase | Catches "best it services temecula," "affordable it services temecula" |
| it support temecula | Phrase | Same geo-modifier pattern |
| managed it services temecula | Phrase | Catches "top managed it services temecula ca" |

### Core Service Keywords
| Keyword | Match Type | Reasoning |
|---------|-----------|-----------|
| managed it services | Exact | High-value head term, control every click |
| managed it services provider | Exact | Specific, high-intent |
| cybersecurity for small business | Exact | Clear buyer intent, no need for phrase reach |

## When to Expand Match Types

After 2-4 weeks of data with exact match:

1. Review the search term report
2. If you see exact match is getting limited impressions, consider adding phrase variants of your top performers
3. If phrase match is pulling in good queries you hadn't thought of, promote them to exact and keep the phrase running
4. Only move to broad if the account has a mature negative keyword list and the budget can absorb the waste
