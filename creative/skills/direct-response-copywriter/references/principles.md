# DR Principles — the timeless layer

The tactics here do **not** change per brand. They are the foundational direct-response
mechanics that `winning-ads.md` examples are specific applications of. When the monthly
distillation pass finds a pattern repeated across three or more winners, the pattern is
written up here as a principle.

This file is the "taste" the SKILL.md hard rules point at. It never overrides the five hard
rules — it fills in the judgment around them.

---

## The 5 awareness stages (Schwartz) — pick one before writing

The reader's awareness stage decides where the copy *starts*, not just what it says.

| Stage | What they know | Where the copy opens |
|---|---|---|
| **Unaware** | No sense of the problem | Open on the symptom / the story. Never lead with the offer. |
| **Problem-aware** | Feels the pain, doesn't know solutions exist | Agitate the problem, then reveal the category of solution. |
| **Solution-aware** | Knows solutions exist, comparing types | Lead with the mechanism — why *this kind* of solution wins. |
| **Product-aware** | Knows your product, not convinced | Lead with proof, offer, risk-reversal. |
| **Most-aware** | Ready, needs a reason now | Lead with the offer + urgency. Short. |

A mismatch here is the #1 cause of hollow copy: leading with the offer to a problem-aware reader who doesn't yet know they need it.

## Market sophistication → how loud the mechanism has to be

(Mirrors SKILL.md §3.) The more players making the same claim, the more the *mechanism* — not the claim — carries the ad.

- **Low:** bare outcome works. ("Save 30% on your roof.")
- **Rising:** outcome + a bigger/faster version of the claim.
- **High:** outcome **+ mechanism** required. The unique reason you can credibly claim this. ("One-day roof replacements — because we overstaff six in-house crew, not two subcontractors.")
- **Saturated:** mechanism becomes the headline; reframe the whole problem (see Contrarian angle).

## Resistance type → register (the upstream call)

(Mirrors SKILL.md §4.) Diagnose offer + audience *together*.

- **Functional resistance** — "is it real, prove it, why you." Reader is evaluating. → Claims, stats, mechanism, proof. Punchy, transactional.
- **Emotional resistance** — "can I trust this, will I be judged/rushed/safe." Reader is blocked by feeling. → Dissolve the block first with a warm runway + founder anchor, *then* the functional argument.
- A functional offer to an emotional audience still needs the warm runway. An emotional offer to a functional audience still needs the proof.

## The 8 angles — expanded

(Mirrors SKILL.md's angle table; use this for the "when to reach for it" call.)

| Angle | Reach for it when… |
|---|---|
| **Pain point** | The pain is acute and top-of-mind; problem-aware reader. |
| **Outcome** | Low sophistication; the result alone is still novel. |
| **Social proof** | Trust/risk is the main blocker; you have real numbers or names. |
| **Curiosity** | Feed/scroll context; you need the stop before you can make the argument. |
| **Comparison** | Solution-aware reader actively weighing alternatives. |
| **Urgency** | Most-aware reader who just needs a reason to act now. Never fake it. |
| **Identity** | Sharp niche; "this is for *you specifically*" beats any feature. |
| **Contrarian** | Saturated market; the reader has misdiagnosed their own problem. |

## Structural principles (distilled from winners — add as patterns prove out)

- **The body copy is a mini sales letter.** It carries its own hook, architecture, and close — it never leans on the headline having done setup (SKILL.md hard rule 4).
- **Lift verbatim customer language** from the research brief. Real words beat clever words.
- **One big idea per ad.** If a line doesn't serve it, cut it.
